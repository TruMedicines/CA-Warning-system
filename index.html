Weather Underground runs a network of community-owned personal weather stations (PWS), often just blocks from you. Readings are not professionally calibrated, so treat them as a neighborhood-level check against the official NWS data above.

Active fire perimeters within ~75 miles. Areas inside or downhill of a burn scar carry extreme flash-flood and debris-flow risk during heavy rain.

Checking active fire perimeters…

Caltrans QuickMap is California's official source for road conditions, closures, chain controls and live camera snapshots. Caltrans recommends linking to QuickMap rather than embedding it, so these are direct links. In QuickMap, open the Layers menu, turn on cameras and chain controls, and zoom to trouble spots like I-80 over Donner Summit, US-50 at Echo Summit, I-5 over the Grapevine, SR-58 at Tehachapi Pass, and I-15 at Cajon Pass. You can also call Caltrans highway information at (800) 427-7623.

No single official statewide feed covers every California utility, so pick yours for its live outage map. Municipal utilities and co-ops publish their own maps; if yours isn't listed, search its name plus “outage map”. During high fire danger, utilities may also cut power on purpose (Public Safety Power Shutoffs), so check yours for PSPS updates.

Published once a year, not live data — a long-range, folklore-influenced seasonal outlook, not a meteorological forecast. Useful for rough planning, not for a specific storm. Last updated in this page: October 2026, covering Winter 2026–2027.

The Old Farmer's Almanac Winter 2026–27
Calls for a warmer-than-normal and wetter winter, helped along by a strong El Niño, which often favors a wetter West. California spans two Almanac regions. In the Pacific Southwest, mountain areas should see near- to above-normal snowfall, with the coldest stretches in late December, mid-to-late January, early February, and late March, and the stormiest periods in late December, mid-January, early and late February, and early and late March. The Pacific Northwest region, covering the north, also expects above-normal precipitation and snowfall, with the coldest snaps in late December, early January, and early February.
